# Prediction-Market AMM and Market-Making Design - Uniform-Loss and Optimal Quoting

## Citation / Link

Ciamac C. Moallemi, Dan Robinson, Brian Zhu, “Uniform-Loss Automated Market Making for Prediction Markets,” arXiv:2607.17428v2, updated 2026-07-23. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17428v2

Dominik Feil, Max Nendel, “Optimal Market Making in Prediction Markets,” arXiv:2607.17991v1, 2026-07-20. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17991v1

## Summary

Two new prediction-market microstructure papers focus on liquidity provision under binary settlement. The uniform-loss AMM paper studies how market-maker subsidy losses are distributed across price states and time using a loss-versus-rebalancing lens. It introduces uniform AMMs where instantaneous LVR is proportional to pool value and independent of the current token price, with dynamic liquidity management to target an expected cumulative loss schedule.

The optimal market-making paper develops a stochastic-control framework where the prediction-market price is a conditional probability generated by a transformed latent belief diffusion. The market maker selects bid/ask quotes to maximize expected terminal wealth while controlling mark-to-market inventory risk and settlement risk at resolution. The abstract emphasizes that binary settlement creates an optimization problem materially different from classical market making.

## Core Contribution

- Adds liquidity-provider economics to the library’s prediction-market thread, complementing settlement manipulation, structural volatility, and DePM taxonomy notes.
- Separates ordinary bid/ask inventory risk from binary resolution risk and time-to-settlement exposure.
- Suggests that prediction-market liquidity should be modeled as a function of probability level, time-to-resolution, belief volatility, fee/subsidy mechanism, AMM design, and inventory/settlement risk.
- Provides a framework for evaluating whether observed wide spreads or thin liquidity are rational compensation for binary settlement risk rather than exploitable inefficiency.

## Practical Relevance

- Classification: **Evidence-backed at abstract level as market-design / market-making theory; foundational**.
- Practicality: **foundational / retail-adaptable** if the user later obtains prediction-market quote/trade data; otherwise not immediately tradable.
- Not direct evidence for retail market-making alpha. Many venues have wide spreads, fees, API/execution frictions, uncertain resolution rules, and inventory caps.
- Useful for designing risk filters around prediction-market settlement-window, structural-volatility, and liquidity-provision studies.

## Methods and Data

Abstract-level details:

- Uniform-loss AMM: loss-versus-rebalancing, win-martingale processes, pricing functions that equalize LVR, and dynamic liquidity schedules.
- Optimal quoting: stochastic control, latent belief diffusion transformed into conditional event probability, HJB equation, unique optimal bid/ask quotes, and numerical analysis.
- Both papers are theoretical/methodological; no retail execution sample is validated from the abstracts.

## Leakage / Bias / Overfitting Concerns

- Theoretical assumptions about belief dynamics or win-martingales may not match real event markets with news jumps, manipulation, resolution disputes, or stale quotes.
- Backtests can leak if the resolution outcome or near-resolution information is implicitly encoded in features.
- Profitability claims from quoted spreads must include queue priority, adverse selection, fees, failed/canceled orders, inventory financing, position limits, and resolution risk.
- Market-design parameters change quickly across Polymarket/Kalshi/DePM-style venues.

## Transaction Cost / Capacity Treatment

Transaction costs are central: bid/ask spreads are compensation for inventory and resolution risk, while AMM LVR/subsidy losses represent liquidity-provider cost. Any retail adaptation should assume poor fills, limited queue priority, venue fees, and event-resolution/legal risk.

## Strategy Ideas Extracted

Do not code a prediction-market market-making strategy yet. If data become available, start with diagnostics:

1. Tag each contract by probability bucket, time-to-resolution, event category, venue, settlement rule, spread, depth/volume, and fee schedule.
2. Estimate realized spread capture versus adverse selection and resolution jumps.
3. Compare simple quote-avoidance/risk filters against structural volatility and near-resolution avoidance.
4. Treat settlement-window reversals as a risk-control/event-study first, not a market-making alpha claim.
