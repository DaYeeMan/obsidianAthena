# Event: Vertiv raises 2026 guidance on AI/datacenter infrastructure demand

## Facts

- Vertiv filed a Form 8-K on July 29, 2026 furnishing Exhibit 99.1 with Q2 2026 results.
- Vertiv reported Q2 net sales of $3.274 billion, up 24% year over year, including 18% organic sales growth.
- Q2 operating profit increased 44% year over year to $638 million, and adjusted operating profit increased 51% to $738 million.
- Adjusted operating margin was 22.6%, up 410 bps year over year, driven by operational execution, productivity, and favorable price-cost, inclusive of tariff impacts and countermeasures.
- Vertiv said Q2 revenue reflected minor timing shifts, primarily due to temporary supply-chain congestion and multi-phased project execution as deployments scale in size and complexity.
- Management said demand for AI and general compute continues to intensify, deployments are becoming more infrastructure-intensive, pipelines continue to strengthen globally, and capacity expansions are underway to serve customers.
- Vertiv raised full-year 2026 guidance across key metrics. At the midpoint, guidance implies net sales of $14.0 billion and organic sales growth of 31% versus full-year 2025, adjusted EPS of $6.70, and adjusted free cash flow of $2.5 billion.
- Q3 2026 guidance calls for net sales of $3.65-3.85 billion and organic growth of 34%-36%.
- The release says full-year 2026 capex is expected to be approximately 4.0% of revenue, at the high end of the range, as Vertiv invests to support demand.

## Affected supply-chain nodes

- Datacenter power and thermal infrastructure: UPS, power distribution, thermal management, modular infrastructure, services, and spares.
- AI datacenter construction and deployment timing: Vertiv explicitly tied minor revenue timing shifts to temporary supply-chain congestion and multi-phased execution as deployments scale.
- Supplier read-through: electrical/cooling peers and adjacent buildout suppliers including Eaton, Schneider Electric, ABB, Quanta Services, Modine, AI server OEMs, and large datacenter developers.

## Interpretation

- The filing is a primary, same-day supplier-side confirmation that AI/datacenter physical infrastructure demand remains strong enough to support higher revenue, margin, cash-flow, and capacity-investment expectations.
- The supply-chain signal is mixed but important: demand and pricing/margin execution are strong, while temporary congestion and complex multi-phase deployments show physical infrastructure and execution capacity are still bottleneck layers.
- This updates the model from mostly lease/MW/developer evidence toward direct power/cooling supplier fundamentals: the bottleneck is translating into revenue growth, margin expansion, and supplier capex, not just customer commitments.
