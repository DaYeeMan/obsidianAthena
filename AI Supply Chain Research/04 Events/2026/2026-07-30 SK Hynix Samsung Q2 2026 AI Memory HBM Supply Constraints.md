# Event: SK Hynix and Samsung Q2 2026 results confirm AI memory/HBM supply constraints

## Facts

- SK Hynix published Q2 2026 results on July 29, 2026. The company reported revenue of KRW 79.3187 trillion, operating profit of KRW 60.5426 trillion, and operating margin of 76%, each framed as record quarterly performance.
- SK Hynix said revenue and operating profit increased 257% and 557% year over year, respectively, driven by sustained demand growth from expanding AI infrastructure investments and price increases for high-performance AI-server products.
- SK Hynix said both DRAM and NAND prices increased significantly quarter over quarter, and that profitability was supported by sales mix centered on HBM, AI-server DRAM, and eSSD.
- SK Hynix said major technology companies' AI infrastructure investments continue to generate additional supply requests, and that in an environment where customer demand exceeds supply capabilities, timely volume delivery has become a core business-competitiveness factor.
- SK Hynix said it is expanding multi-year contract discussions with customers for supply stability and has long-term agreements with around 10 key customers.
- SK Hynix said HBM4 achieved customer-required operating speeds, industry-leading power efficiency, and cost competitiveness; mass shipments of HBM4 began in Q2 2026 and production will ramp in H2 2026.
- SK Hynix said HBM4E sample shipments were completed in H1 2026, and that it is accelerating M15X mass production while preparing capacity expansion after the Yongin Phase 1 cleanroom opens in early 2027.
- Samsung Electronics published Q2 2026 results on July 30, 2026. Samsung reported KRW 171.5 trillion of consolidated revenue and KRW 89.2 trillion of operating profit.
- Samsung said its Device Solutions division posted KRW 127.5 trillion of consolidated revenue and KRW 89.7 trillion of operating profit, with the Memory Business setting all-time highs for quarterly revenue and operating profit.
- Samsung said the Memory Business addressed AI demand despite limited capacity, focused primarily on server products, and achieved a record-high server share of the sales mix.
- Samsung said it scaled up HBM4 sales and shipped the industry's first HBM4E samples to major customers.
- Samsung said H2 2026 memory demand should remain robust around servers, AI infrastructure capex, and agentic AI adoption, while growth in demand for server DRAM, eSSDs, and HBM is expected to accelerate; despite efforts to increase production, supply constraints are expected to continue.
- Samsung said Foundry earnings improved significantly before incentive-related provisions, driven by HBM base-die demand and strong U.S. and China customer orders; for H2 2026 it plans to expand 4nm LPU and base-die sales and ramp second-generation 2nm mobile products.
- This event was discovered and captured on August 1, 2026 from primary company releases within the active monitoring window.

- HBM4/HBM4E qualification, shipment ramp, and customer allocation.

- Accelerator/platform vendors dependent on HBM availability and power-efficient memory roadmaps.

## Interpretation

- The combined primary evidence moves the HBM bottleneck model from isolated product/partnership evidence toward supplier-fundamental confirmation: both leading Korean memory suppliers say AI infrastructure demand is converting into record memory earnings, HBM4 sales/shipments, HBM4E sampling, multi-year customer discussions, and continuing supply constraints.
- This is stronger than generic AI-demand commentary because the suppliers disclosed supply-demand imbalance language, pricing/mix uplift, HBM4/HBM4E milestones, and customer-contract/supply-stability mechanisms.
- The evidence supports the view that memory/HBM availability and pricing remain a binding or near-binding constraint for next-generation AI platforms even as foundry, packaging, power, and datacenter construction also remain bottlenecks.
