# Event: Atkore agrees to be acquired by Prysmian as AI-driven datacenter electrification demand consolidates suppliers

## Facts

- Atkore filed two Form 8-Ks on August 3, 2026, including Exhibit 99.1 announcing that Atkore entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Prysmian S.p.A. in an all-cash transaction.
- Transaction terms: Atkore shareholders will receive $95.00 per share in cash; Atkore said the transaction represents an enterprise value of approximately $3.8 billion.
- Atkore said the price represented a 30% premium to its July 31, 2026 closing price and 57% to the September 29, 2025 closing price before Atkore announced its strategic review.
- Atkore describes itself as a leading manufacturer of electrical infrastructure products.
- Prysmian's CEO said: "Electrification, AI-driven data centers and digitalization all require major investments in infrastructure," and framed Atkore as complementary products plus structural-growth exposure that accelerate Prysmian into a fuller electrical-solutions provider.
- Atkore and Prysmian boards unanimously approved the transaction; closing is targeted by calendar year-end 2026, subject to Atkore shareholder approval, regulatory approvals, and customary closing conditions.
- Prysmian expects to fund the transaction with a mix of debt, including hybrid bonds, and equity, including treasury-share disposal, while targeting preservation of its investment-grade profile.
- In a separate August 3, 2026 earnings 8-K, Atkore reported fiscal Q3 2026 net sales of $794.8 million, up 8.1% year over year; Electrical segment net sales of $578.3 million, up 10.9%; adjusted net income per diluted share of $1.92, up $0.29 year over year; and adjusted EBITDA of $104.7 million, up $4.7 million year over year.

## Interpretation

- This is not a new MW lease, GPU contract, or capacity-delivery milestone. The supply-chain relevance is supplier consolidation: a global cable/electrical-systems company is paying a control premium for a U.S. electrical-infrastructure manufacturer while explicitly citing AI-driven datacenter infrastructure investment as part of the strategic rationale.
- The event adds M&A/strategic-buyer evidence to the existing Vertiv/Eaton supplier-side earnings evidence that AI datacenter buildout is changing value and competitive positioning in electrical infrastructure, not just demand for GPUs or cloud services.
- The transaction also reduces ATKR as a standalone public-market exposure if it closes, while increasing Prysmian's relevance as a datacenter-electrification supplier to monitor.
