# Event: AMD Q2 2026 results show Data Center revenue more than doubled as Instinct deployments and Helios ramp

## Facts

- AMD filed a Form 8-K on August 4, 2026 furnishing Exhibit 99.1 Q2 2026 results and Exhibit 99.2 earnings slides.
- Q2 revenue was a record $11.536 billion, up 50% year over year and 13% sequentially.
- GAAP gross margin was 54%, up 14 percentage points year over year; non-GAAP gross margin was 56%, up 13 percentage points year over year.
- CEO Lisa Su said AMD delivered record revenue and profitability as Data Center revenue more than doubled year over year, with EPYC demand accelerating, Instinct deployments scaling, and Helios beginning to ramp.
- CFO Jean Hu said Data Center represented 58% of company revenue in the quarter and that AMD expects Data Center sales to accelerate in the second half of 2026.
- Segment disclosure in the release says Data Center revenue was $6.7 billion, up 107% year over year, driven by strong demand for AMD EPYC processors and AMD Instinct GPUs.
- The release lists the AMD Helios rackscale solution and Instinct MI350P GPU among recent AI computing launches.
- The SEC-furnished earnings slides include forward-looking partnership language referencing a strategic partnership with Anthropic and deployment of up to two gigawatts of AMD Instinct GPUs in AMD Helios racks.

## Affected supply-chain nodes

- Merchant AI accelerators: AMD Instinct demand and Helios rackscale deployments now have primary earnings evidence of revenue conversion.
- AI server/rack integration: Helios rackscale ramp increases the importance of rack-level integration, cooling, networking, and power delivery, not just GPU unit demand.
- Datacenter capacity: this reinforces the July Core Scientific / AMD event in which AMD secured more than 500 MW / about 530 MW of U.S. AI-ready infrastructure beginning in 2027.
- HBM/foundry/advanced packaging: higher AMD accelerator deployment visibility implies additional demand for HBM, leading-edge foundry, and packaging capacity, but AMD did not disclose GPU units, HBM attach, package allocation, or supplier mix.

## Interpretation

- This is stronger than a generic product update because the filing ties AI accelerator demand to realized Data Center revenue, margin recovery, second-half acceleration expectations, Helios rackscale ramp, and large-scale partnership language.
- The disclosure supports the thesis that AMD is moving from product-roadmap optionality toward deployable AI infrastructure demand, especially when paired with the Core Scientific capacity-reservation event.
- The main uncertainty is magnitude by accelerator generation: AMD did not quantify Instinct GPU revenue, MI350/Helios units, Anthropic timing, HBM consumption, wafer/package allocation, or the share of Data Center growth attributable to EPYC versus Instinct.

## Investment / quant relevance

### Discretionary catalyst angle

- AMD's Data Center revenue acceleration and Helios ramp can change perceived competitive positioning versus NVIDIA and custom silicon, while also creating read-through demand for HBM suppliers, TSMC/advanced packaging, AI server OEMs/ODMs, and datacenter power/cooling suppliers.
- The signal should be interpreted alongside the Core Scientific / AMD datacenter capacity agreement: AMD appears to be pairing accelerator/rack offerings with control over deployable power-rich capacity for customers.

### Quant-testable hypothesis

- Universe: AMD, NVDA, TSM, HBM suppliers, AI server OEMs/ODMs, networking, and datacenter power/cooling suppliers.
- Signal: primary accelerator-vendor earnings where Data Center revenue grows >75% YoY and management discloses AI accelerator/rackscale deployment acceleration or MW-scale customer infrastructure partnerships.
- Target variable: direct and supplier-basket excess returns, estimate revisions, and later backlog/order growth.
- Expected lead/lag: 0-5 trading days for direct and supplier read-through; 1-2 quarters for HBM, server, power/cooling, and foundry/packaging conversion.
- Data requirements: filing timestamp, segment revenue/growth, management deployment language, disclosed MW/rackscale fields, consensus revisions, supplier exposure map, and later shipment/backlog confirmations.

## Confidence and uncertainty

- Confidence: high that AMD disclosed record Q2 revenue, Data Center revenue of $6.7 billion up 107% YoY, Instinct/Helios demand language, and second-half Data Center acceleration expectations in SEC-furnished materials.
- What remains uncertain: Instinct GPU units, platform-generation mix, HBM generation/supplier allocation, TSMC/package capacity allocation, Anthropic deployment schedule, customer concentration, and whether Helios converts into sustained share gains versus NVIDIA and hyperscaler custom silicon.
