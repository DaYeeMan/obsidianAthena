# Event: TeraWulf and Galaxy Q2 results show AI datacenter capacity moving from leases into delivered load, revenue, and new powered-site pipelines

## Facts

- TeraWulf filed a Form 8-K on August 5, 2026 with Exhibit 99.1 reporting Q2 2026 results and development updates.
- TeraWulf reported Q2 revenue of $44.8 million, including $31.9 million of HPC lease revenue, or about 71% of total revenue.
- TeraWulf said it operated 81 MW of revenue-generating critical IT capacity at Lake Mariner as of June 30, 2026 and completed delivery of CB-3 in early July, increasing revenue-generating capacity to 102 MW.
- The CB-3 delivery satisfied conditions for $600 million of Google's credit support for Fluidstack lease obligations to become effective.
- TeraWulf said another 336 MW across CB-4 and CB-5 remains under construction, with the first CB-4 data hall in commissioning, phased delivery and rent commencement expected during 2H 2026, and CB-5 targeted for phased delivery in early 2027.
- TeraWulf acquired the Muskie Data Campus in eastern Kentucky and entered electric service / infrastructure agreements with Kentucky Power Company for up to 1 GW of contracted electric service.
- TeraWulf's subsequent-event section reiterates the previously captured 20-year Anthropic lease for about 401 MW of critical IT capacity at Justified, about $19 billion of initial-term contracted revenue, and Google support for Anthropic's lease obligations.
- TeraWulf agreed to monetize its Abernathy joint-venture interest for about $530 million and reaffirmed a target of contracting 250-500 MW of incremental critical IT capacity annually.
- Galaxy Digital filed a Form 8-K on August 5, 2026 with Exhibit 99.1 and Exhibit 99.2 reporting Q2 2026 results and datacenter updates.
- Galaxy said Phase I at Helios delivered 200 MW of gross power / 133 MW of critical IT load to CoreWeave under a 15-year lease, with rent commencement scaling with delivered capacity during Q2 2026; all 133 MW was in service by quarter-end.
- Galaxy said Phase I is expected to generate quarterly leasing revenue of approximately $80 million and project-level adjusted EBITDA margin above 90% beginning in Q3 2026.
- Galaxy completed the previously captured $3.5 billion Helios Phase II senior secured note offering on July 28, 2026 to fund construction of the 260 MW critical-IT-load CoreWeave Phase II project.
- Galaxy also disclosed post-quarter expansion of its Texas AI datacenter footprint to more than 5.7 GW of potential power capacity: Merlin in McGregor, Texas with 74 MW initially and potential to expand to 500 MW, plus Caspian and Selene sites with potential power capacities of about 700 MW and 900 MW, respectively, subject to ERCOT interconnection and other development conditions.

## Affected supply-chain nodes

- AI datacenter developers / power-rich former miners: WULF and GLXY move further from lease announcements toward operating critical IT load, recurring lease revenue, and funded construction.
- AI demand aggregators and tenants: Fluidstack / Google-supported Lake Mariner, Anthropic at Justified, and CoreWeave at Helios.
- Datacenter power and construction suppliers: delivered and under-construction MW support demand read-through for electrical equipment, cooling, construction services, commissioning labor, and high-density datacenter infrastructure suppliers.
- Utility / grid layer: Kentucky Power and ERCOT/Texas interconnection exposure remain critical gating items for the Muskie, Merlin, Caspian, Selene, and Helios expansion pipelines.

## Interpretation

- The highest-quality incremental signal is not another future lease headline; it is conversion of previously signed AI/HPC commitments into delivered critical IT load, lease revenue, and credit-support activation.
- TeraWulf's 102 MW online and 336 MW under construction, plus Galaxy's 133 MW online with expected $80 million quarterly Phase I leasing revenue, strengthen confidence that some power-rich developer portfolios are becoming operating datacenter infrastructure rather than only speculative powered land.
- New powered-site pipelines remain lower certainty than delivered load or signed leases. Galaxy's >5.7 GW potential pipeline and TeraWulf's 1 GW Muskie service agreements are material for future supply, but tenanting, interconnection approval, financing, equipment procurement, and construction execution still need validation.

## Investment / quant relevance

### Discretionary catalyst angle

- Direct: WULF and GLXY have clearer evidence of operating AI/HPC datacenter revenue and development execution; future catalyst checks should emphasize delivered MW, rent commencement, credit-support activation, construction cost per MW, and debt terms rather than only announced gross pipeline.
- Read-through: VRT, ETN, SU.PA, ABB, PWR, MOD and other power/cooling/construction suppliers may benefit if delivered-load and under-construction MW pipelines continue to convert into orders/backlog.

### Quant-testable hypothesis

- Universe: Power-rich AI datacenter developers/former miners, datacenter infrastructure suppliers, AI server OEMs, and hyperscaler/GPU-cloud counterparties.
- Signal: Primary 8-K/earnings disclosures of delivered critical IT load, lease-rent commencement, credit-support activation, contracted electric service, and >=500 MW powered-site pipeline additions.
- Target variable: 0-5 day direct/peer returns; 1-4 quarter changes in revenue, NOI, backlog, capex, and supplier estimate revisions.
- Expected lead/lag: Immediate for direct developer repricing; lagged for supplier conversion and tenant-capacity absorption.
- Data requirements: SEC filing timestamps, MW fields, delivered/under-construction/pipeline classification, tenant/customer identity, lease term and credit support, financing cost, construction cost per MW, and follow-on commissioning milestones.

## Confidence and uncertainty

- Confidence: high that the disclosed operational and development facts are primary-source evidence.
- What remains uncertain: Whether TeraWulf's Muskie and Galaxy's Merlin/Caspian/Selene pipelines secure tenants and interconnection approvals; whether cost/MW and financing terms remain within guidance; how much supplier backlog is directly attributable to these campuses; and whether lease credit-support terms are fully durable through construction and ramp.
