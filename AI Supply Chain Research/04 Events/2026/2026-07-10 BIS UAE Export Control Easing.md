# Event: BIS eases export controls for UAE advanced computing items

## Facts

- On July 10, 2026, the U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security announced that it would significantly upgrade the United Arab Emirates' status under the Export Administration Regulations.
- BIS said it will remove the UAE from EAR Country Groups D:3 and D:4 and reclassify the UAE as Country Group A:5.
- BIS said this change makes the UAE Government and approved commercial entities eligible for license-free exports, reexports, and in-country transfers under License Exception Strategic Trade Authorization for specified Commerce-controlled military, satellite/spacecraft, and dual-use items available to A:5 destinations.
- BIS also said that, consistent with the U.S.-UAE Artificial Intelligence Cooperation framework signed in May 2025, Commerce is approving the UAE Government and certain companies to receive advanced computing items in the UAE license-free, including AI chips and servers.
- BIS linked the approval to UAE investment commitments under the framework, including matching investments in U.S. AI digital infrastructure buildout.
- The announcement points to a Federal Register notice implementing the regulatory changes; the notice was available for public inspection via Federal Register document link 2026-14132 at discovery time.

## Affected supply-chain nodes

- Export-control policy for advanced computing items.
- AI accelerator and server demand tied to approved UAE government/commercial recipients.
- AI datacenter infrastructure buildout in both the UAE and the U.S. if matching-investment commitments convert into deployed capacity.

## Interpretation

- This is a directional easing of U.S. export controls for an allied Gulf AI infrastructure market, not a blanket global relaxation.
- The event potentially increases addressable demand for AI chips and servers outside China while preserving an approval/approved-entity framework.
- The public-market impact depends on which entities are approved, the volumes and product classes covered, compliance guardrails, and whether announced investment commitments become actual datacenter capacity and equipment orders.
