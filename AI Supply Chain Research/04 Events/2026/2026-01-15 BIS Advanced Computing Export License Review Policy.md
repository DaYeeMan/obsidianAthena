# Event: BIS revises license review policy for advanced computing commodities

## Facts

- The U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security published a Federal Register rule on January 15, 2026 titled "Revision to License Review Policy for Advanced Computing Commodities."
- The rule changed BIS license review policy for exports of certain semiconductors to China and Macau from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review.
- The Federal Register abstract says the covered semiconductors include NVIDIA H200 and equivalents, as well as less advanced chips, subject to conditions.
- The stated conditions include exporter certification that there is sufficient U.S. supply, that exports will not divert global foundry capacity for similar or more advanced U.S.-end-user products, that the recipient has demonstrated sufficient security procedures, and that the item undergoes independent third-party U.S. testing to verify performance specifications.
- Publication date and effective date are both January 15, 2026.
- This note is a historical database-gap addition discovered on 2026-07-11; no Discord alert was sent solely because the event was not newly announced today.

## Interpretation

- This is a primary-sourced policy shift that could ease some restricted-chip export pathways while adding certification, supply-sufficiency, security, and testing gates.
- The rule does not by itself prove license approvals, shipment volumes, or revenue recapture for NVIDIA or peers; those require company disclosure, license data, customs/import signals, or channel evidence.
- The explicit foundry-capacity certification condition makes the policy relevant beyond China revenue: BIS is tying export review to whether shipments would divert capacity from U.S. or allied advanced-computing demand.
