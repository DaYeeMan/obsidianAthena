# Event: Galaxy delivers 133 MW of critical IT load to CoreWeave at Helios Phase I

## Facts

- Galaxy announced on July 6, 2026 that it completed delivery of Phase I power at its Helios data center campus in West Texas.
- The company says Phase I delivered approximately 200 MW of gross power, equal to 133 MW of critical IT load, to CoreWeave under a 15-year lease agreement.
- Galaxy says Phase I was delivered on schedule, with rent commencement under the Phase I lease beginning in Q2 2026; Helios has therefore transitioned into revenue-generating operations.
- Galaxy says greenfield development is underway on the 260 MW critical-IT-load Phase II build, with Phase II data hall deliveries expected to commence in 1H 2027.
- Across Phases I-III, Galaxy says CoreWeave has committed to 526 MW of critical IT load, representing the full 800 MW of gross power currently approved and contracted at Helios, under 15-year leases with two five-year extension options.
- Galaxy says the CoreWeave leases are expected to generate more than $1 billion in average annual revenue.
- Galaxy also says Helios's total approved power capacity has expanded to 1.63 GW, with potential to scale to as much as 3.6 GW.
- Galaxy identifies itself as Galaxy Digital Inc. (Nasdaq: GLXY). CoreWeave is public as CRWV.

## Interpretation

- This is not just a generic datacenter announcement: it is a primary-sourced operational delivery milestone with MW, tenant, lease duration, rent commencement, follow-on delivery schedule, and revenue expectations.
- Together with the TeraWulf/Anthropic lease, it strengthens the current model that power-ready campuses and datacenter execution are becoming a key conversion layer between AI demand and usable compute capacity.
- The event is also a direct public-market exposure for Galaxy (GLXY) and CoreWeave (CRWV), while indirect read-throughs apply to electrical/cooling suppliers and construction contractors.
