# Event: MARA acquires HIF Matagorda powered land site for up to 2 GW digital infrastructure campus

## Facts

- MARA filed an 8-K on July 9, 2026 disclosing that subsidiary Volt Texas, LLC entered into and closed a Membership Interest Purchase Agreement with HIF USA LLC on July 2, 2026.
- The acquired project company holds rights under land purchase contracts, title to an adjacent owned parcel, and rights under a letter agreement with an electric utility relating to provision of 2,000 MW of power capacity to the Matagorda County, Texas site.
- MARA's Exhibit 99.1 says the site covers more than 1,200 acres approximately 90 miles southwest of Houston.
- The press release says the site is expected to provide access to up to an initial 1 GW of grid capacity by October 2027 and up to 2 GW by April 2028.
- MARA says the site has received interest from potential high-performance-computing tenants and that it intends to develop the site through its Starwood Digital Ventures partnership as a large-scale digital infrastructure campus for HPC workloads and flexible compute operations, including Bitcoin mining.
- The 8-K says aggregate purchase consideration is structured as post-closing milestone payments tied to project-development events including regulatory approvals/site acquisition, power authorization, and execution of a third-party data-center lease; if all milestones are achieved, aggregate purchase price would be $600 million.
- HIF will retain a minority interest in the site upon execution of a data-center lease with a third-party tenant.
- This event was discovered as a historical database gap on July 18, 2026; no Discord alert was sent solely because the cron discovered it after announcement date.
- MARA's August 6, 2026 Q2 shareholder letter framed AI infrastructure as constrained by power-ready sites, said the Matagorda rights plus existing/expanded Hannibal capacity are expected to expand MARA's power portfolio up to 4.8 GW, and said lease discussions are progressing across multiple sites with management remaining confident it can sign at least one lease before year-end.

## Affected supply-chain nodes

- Datacenter power/site pipeline: adds a primary-sourced multi-GW Texas powered-land datapoint to the AI/HPC campus development queue.
- Demand/cloud: not yet a signed AI tenant, but potential HPC tenant interest and Starwood Digital Ventures development partnership make it relevant to public-market monitoring of compute-campus demand.
- Electrical/cooling/construction suppliers: if leased and built, the site could create future demand for grid interconnection, switchyard/substation work, transformers, switchgear, cooling, racks, and construction services.

## Interpretation

- This is a high-materiality pipeline-capacity signal rather than a confirmed AI capacity delivery or lease event. The disclosed 1 GW / 2 GW grid-capacity timeline is large relative to recent MW-scale AI campus leases, but customer, economics, critical IT load, financing, equipment orders, and permitting remain unconfirmed.
- The event reinforces the active model that power-ready or power-entitled sites are becoming scarce strategic assets for AI/HPC deployment and that former bitcoin-mining infrastructure operators are trying to reposition toward contracted AI datacenter campuses.
- Because there is no named HPC tenant or executed lease, it should be tracked as an upstream site-control/power-option event, below the signal quality of TeraWulf/Anthropic, Galaxy/CoreWeave delivery, Applied Digital leases/deliveries, or CleanSpark's signed lease.
- The August 6, 2026 Q2 letter increases confidence that MARA is actively marketing the portfolio for AI/HPC leases and quantifies the broader power platform at up to 4.8 GW, but it does not change the event into a signed-demand datapoint because management only disclosed lease discussions and a target to sign at least one lease before year-end.

## Investment / quant relevance

### Discretionary catalyst angle

- MARA becomes another direct public exposure to the AI campus lease-and-delivery theme, but the thesis depends on conversion of site/power rights into a signed HPC tenant, financing, and construction milestones.
- Follow-on lease execution, tenant identity, critical IT load, and capital-cost disclosure would be higher signal than this initial site acquisition.

### Quant-testable hypothesis

- Universe: public companies with power-rich land/site-control announcements, former bitcoin miners, datacenter developers, and electrical/cooling suppliers.
- Signal: primary-sourced powered-land or utility-capacity announcements with GW/MW, dates, development partner, and explicit HPC/AI tenant targeting, separated from signed lease and delivered-load milestones.
- Target variable: direct-company abnormal returns, subsequent lease announcements, financing events, and supplier backlog/revenue revisions.
- Expected lead/lag: 0-5 trading days for direct company sentiment; 1-8 quarters for conversion into leases, construction spend, and supplier fundamentals.
- Data requirements: SEC filings/press releases, MW/GW fields, grid-capacity dates, tenant/lease status, capex, financing, interconnection and permitting milestones.

## Confidence and uncertainty

- Confidence: high that MARA disclosed the transaction and the 2 GW power-capacity rights; medium that the site will convert into material AI/HPC deployed capacity because tenant, financing, permitting, equipment, and buildout economics are not yet disclosed.
- What remains uncertain: whether any HPC tenant signs a lease; critical IT load versus gross grid capacity; required capex and funding; regulatory approvals and remaining land acquisition; construction schedule; equipment supplier participation; whether flexible compute/bitcoin mining remains the primary monetization path if HPC leasing is delayed.
