# Event: Meta and Microsoft disclose hyperscaler-scale AI infrastructure capex and lease commitments

## Facts

- Meta filed a Form 8-K on July 29, 2026 with Exhibit 99.1 reporting Q2 2026 results, and filed its Q2 2026 Form 10-Q on July 30, 2026.
- Meta reported Q2 2026 capital expenditures, including principal payments on finance leases, of $31.08 billion.
- Meta narrowed its 2026 capex outlook to $130-145 billion from a prior $125-145 billion range.
- Meta's Q2 2026 Form 10-Q disclosed approximately $278.99 billion of operating and finance lease obligations not yet commenced as of June 30, 2026, consisting of data centers, colocations, and certain network infrastructure, expected to commence during the remainder of 2026 through 2036 with lease terms ranging from greater than one year to 30 years.
- Meta disclosed that in July 2026 it entered additional data-center leases with lease obligations of approximately $68 billion, expected to commence in 2027 and 2028 with lease terms of 18 to 20 years.
- Meta disclosed $349.31 billion of non-cancelable contractual commitments as of June 30, 2026, mostly related to third-party cloud capacity arrangements and investments in servers and network infrastructure, data centers, and Reality Labs consumer-hardware products; $53.52 billion is due in 2026 and $81.65 billion in 2027.
- Meta also disclosed contingent obligations to purchase up to $14.72 billion of cloud capacity over a five-year period.
- Microsoft filed a Form 8-K on July 29, 2026 with Exhibit 99.1 reporting fiscal Q4 2026 results, and filed its fiscal 2026 Form 10-K on July 29, 2026.
- Microsoft reported Q4 Azure and other cloud services revenue growth of 43% year over year and fiscal 2026 Azure and other cloud services revenue growth of 41%.
- Microsoft said fiscal 2026 Microsoft Cloud revenue increased 27% to $214.4 billion and commercial remaining performance obligation increased 84% to $678 billion.
- Microsoft reported fiscal 2026 additions to property and equipment of $115.948 billion, up from $64.551 billion in fiscal 2025, and said cash used in investing increased mainly because of a $51.4 billion increase in additions to property and equipment and a $22.2 billion increase in other investing primarily to facilitate the purchase of components.
- Microsoft disclosed contractual obligations as of June 30, 2026 totaling $743.821 billion, including $34.566 billion of construction commitments, $443.506 billion of operating and finance leases including imputed interest, and $194.060 billion of purchase commitments that primarily relate to datacenters and include open purchase orders and take-or-pay contracts not presented as construction commitments.
- Microsoft said it will continue to invest in capex to support cloud growth and AI training / other infrastructure, including datacenters, computer systems, necessary components, and energy resources.

## Interpretation

- This is not just generic AI product commentary. Meta and Microsoft disclosed balance-sheet-scale AI/cloud infrastructure obligations that are large enough to affect supplier backlogs, datacenter construction pipelines, power/cooling demand, and financing needs.
- Meta's $68 billion of additional July data-center leases is a new primary-disclosed post-quarter commitment and should be tracked against the AI campus lease theme even though tenant/vendor identities, MW, locations, and delivery milestones are mostly undisclosed in the filing.
- Microsoft's contractual obligation stack and 84% commercial RPO growth show AI/cloud demand converting into long-duration capacity, component, lease, and construction commitments, but the filing does not disaggregate AI-specific MW or supplier allocations.
