# Event: Nebius Q2 results raise 2026 contracted power target to 5 GW after AI cloud demand inflection

## Facts

- Nebius Group published Q2 2026 results and a shareholder letter on August 12, 2026.
- Nebius reported Q2 group revenue of $582.3 million, up 454% year over year; Nebius AI Cloud revenue grew 514% year over year to $575 million and annualized run-rate revenue reached $3.0 billion.
- The shareholder letter says Q2 was Nebius's strongest commercial quarter yet: the company closed four landmark AI cloud deals with average total contract value above $1 billion each and annual contract value of $20-$25 million per MW.
- Named Q2 customers include Reflection for a multi-year agreement to train and run open-source models on Nebius, and Cohere for agentic enterprise AI infrastructure using flexible next-generation NVIDIA architectures. Nebius also disclosed a U.S.-based AI neolab and a large U.S.-based quantitative trading firm as additional landmark wins.
- Nebius said TCV of Q2 deals grew nearly 4x quarter over quarter and TCV from new customers grew more than 9x.
- Roughly 70% of Q2 deals included customer prepayments, covering 50%-60% of associated capex; Nebius expects more than $9 billion of customer prepayments in 2026.
- Nebius raised its 2026 contracted-power target to 5 GW, up from more than 4 GW last quarter and more than 1 GW in August 2025. The letter defines contracted power as contracted land and power commitments.
- Nebius said it plans to deploy more than 1 GW per year starting in 2027 and that the majority of Q2 deals were signed against capacity arriving in late 2026, contributing primarily to 2027 revenue.
- Nebius expanded its contracted capacity footprint in Q2 with additional sites in the UK, Estonia, and Finland, and said construction is underway at some owned U.S. AI factories. Its shareholder-letter map also shows capacity footprint points in Minnesota, Kansas City, Pennsylvania, Missouri, New Jersey, Oklahoma, Alabama, Iceland, the UK, France, Spain, Finland, Estonia, and Israel.
- Nebius said buildout supporting its second Meta agreement is on track to come online in early 2027.
- Nebius introduced an asset-light partnership model under which partners deploy Nebius's full-stack AI cloud platform in their own AI datacenters, while Nebius contributes software, systems architecture/reference designs, and go-to-market demand aggregation.
- Nebius reported Q2 capex of approximately $5.7 billion, primarily GPU/GPU-related hardware and datacenter expansion activity. The letter says it had an $8 billion cash position at quarter-end, including $2.3 billion of positive operating cash flow, and secured about $775 million of asset-backed financing in July at SOFR + 2.50%.
- Nebius said it signed its first short-term capacity deal in Q3 for dedicated customer capacity expected to go live in Q4, and piloted a capacity auction that cleared its highest price to date for NVIDIA Blackwell chips.

## Interpretation

- This is a high-signal neo-cloud demand and power-capacity update rather than generic AI product news: Nebius disclosed named AI customers, deal economics per MW, prepayment/capex funding mechanics, Q2 capex, 5 GW contracted-power target, planned >1 GW annual deployments, and specific timing for late-2026 / 2027 capacity conversion.
- The key model change is that public neo-cloud demand is showing hyperscaler-like power-procurement and capex intensity. The 5 GW contracted-power target and >$9B expected customer prepayments suggest that AI cloud procurement is becoming a competing sink for powered datacenter sites and supplier capacity alongside hyperscalers and accelerator-vendor capacity reservations.
- The asset-light model matters because it could convert third-party-owned AI datacenter capacity into Nebius demand without Nebius funding all land/facility/power capex, increasing the need to track partner identity, MW, financing, supplier awards, and operating control.
