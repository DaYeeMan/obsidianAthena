# Foundry, Advanced Packaging, and Substrates

## Role in chain
Leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging convert chip designs into shippable accelerators. ABF substrates, interposers, and packaging equipment can bottleneck shipments.

## Key entities
TSMC, Samsung Foundry, Intel Foundry, ASE, Amkor, Ibiden, Unimicron, AT&S, Shinko, Ajinomoto-related substrate ecosystem.

## Signals to monitor
- CoWoS/SoIC/FoWLP capacity expansion and utilization.
- Leading-edge wafer capacity and allocation.
- ABF substrate lead times/pricing.
- Packaging equipment orders and bottleneck relief.

## Current assessment

TSMC's Q2 2026 release confirms that leading-edge/HPC demand remains strong, with advanced technologies at 77% of wafer revenue and HPC at 66% of net revenue. This supports continued monitoring of leading-edge capacity allocation and equipment demand, but it does not resolve the advanced-packaging/CoWoS bottleneck question because TSMC did not quantify packaging capacity, lead times, or utilization in the public Q2 materials. SK Hynix and Samsung Q2 2026 HBM4/HBM4E evidence reinforces the need to monitor advanced memory stacking, base-die, packaging yield, and related equipment capacity as AI accelerators move to higher memory bandwidth and capacity per package; Samsung's foundry comments specifically tie improved foundry earnings to HBM base-die demand. Export-control rules can also make foundry/packaging capacity allocation an explicit policy condition, so monitor whether license regimes constrain or redirect advanced-capacity availability.
