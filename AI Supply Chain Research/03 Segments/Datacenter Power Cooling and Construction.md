# Datacenter Power, Cooling, and Construction

## Role in chain
Converts chip/server availability into usable AI compute capacity. Can become the binding bottleneck through grid interconnection, transformers, switchgear, cooling, land, water, permitting, and skilled construction labor.

## Key entities
Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric, ABB, Siemens, MOD, PWR, GLXY, APLD, CLSK, MARA, ACS/Turner, BlackRock/GIP-linked platforms where material, datacenter REITs/developers, utilities, grid operators.

## Signals to monitor
- Utility interconnection queues and power purchase agreements.
- Large datacenter campus permits, delays, and cancellations.
- Transformer/switchgear lead times.
- Liquid-cooling adoption and thermal-density constraints.
- Nuclear/gas/renewable power procurement linked to AI datacenters.

## Current assessment

Large AI datacenter commitments are now appearing as primary-sourced, long-duration MW leases, delivery milestones, power-rich site-control transactions, and infrastructure-platform launches with named construction capacity. The TeraWulf/Anthropic 401 MW commitment, Galaxy/CoreWeave 133 MW delivered critical IT load, Applied Digital 210 MW Delta Forge 2 hyperscaler lease, CleanSpark 175 MW Sandersville lease, Applied Digital 75 MW Polaris Forge 1 operational delivery, MARA/HIF Matagorda 2 GW powered-site pipeline, and Coravel/ACS/GIP 140 MW DFW hyperscaler agreement support treating power/site readiness and electrical/cooling/construction capacity as a binding deployment layer for frontier AI compute. Execution risk remains high for future phases until financing, equipment, interconnection, tenant leases, and commissioning milestones are visible; secondary-source platform claims should be weighted below SEC-filed lease or delivered-load evidence.
