# Policy, Geopolitics, and Logistics

## Role in chain
Regulation and logistics can reroute demand, constrain supply, alter inventory, and drive local substitution.

## Key entities
US BIS, Dutch/Japanese export-control authorities, China regulators, Taiwan/Korea/Japan industrial policy bodies, customs/logistics providers, affected public companies across the chain.

## Signals to monitor
- Export-control rules, entity-list updates, and license-policy changes.
- China-local accelerator substitution and stockpiling behavior.
- Taiwan geopolitical risk, energy/water constraints, earthquakes/typhoons.
- Tariffs, customs, shipping, and trade-route disruptions.

## Current assessment

U.S. advanced-computing export-control policy remains an active demand-allocation variable. The January 2026 BIS rule indicates a less absolute review stance for covered H200-class exports to China/Macau, while the July 2026 UAE action shows a more permissive path for approved Gulf recipients of AI chips and servers. Actual investment impact depends on approved entities, shipment volumes, product scope, compliance guardrails, and whether infrastructure commitments convert into deployed datacenter capacity.
